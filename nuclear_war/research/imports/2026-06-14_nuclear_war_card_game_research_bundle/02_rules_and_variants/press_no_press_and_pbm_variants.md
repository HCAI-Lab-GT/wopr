# Press, no-press, and postal/PBM variants

## Press source

The strongest press source found is Richard Smith's 1998 postal rules hosted by Variable Pig.

## Press variant model

Press is a public diplomacy layer used between mechanical turns/order cycles. It may include:

- public treaty announcements;
- warnings and deterrent statements;
- threats;
- false claims or bluffing;
- public accusations;
- humor/libel/roleplay statements;
- information operations meant to influence targeting.

Press should not automatically alter card legality unless the PBM GM rules explicitly incorporate it into order adjudication.

## Postal/PBM modifications

For postal play, the game uses mostly simultaneous processing. The source recommends:

- 2 to 8 players;
- cards from *Nuclear War*, *Nuclear Escalation*, and *Nuclear Proliferation* may be used;
- no trading;
- no special powers;
- remove or modify miss-a-turn secrets;
- secrets/top secrets are announced and often resolve on the following turn to enable spy/theft interactions;
- final strike is delayed to the following turn;
- propaganda is processed in a separate phase and fails if nuclear weapons are launched in the relevant cycle;
- press is actively encouraged.

## No-press house variant

No official source labeled "No Press Nuclear War" was found. The clean simulation model is:

- disable public press messages;
- optionally disable private negotiation;
- keep all mechanical turn and attack rules unchanged;
- record only public game events generated by rules.

## AI/agent implications

- In press mode, agents may negotiate, bluff, threaten, promise not to strike, coordinate propaganda, or attempt deterrence.
- In no-press mode, agents act only from visible game state and private hand information.
- In PBM mode, order commitments are simultaneous, which reduces the advantage of reacting to immediate turn order.
