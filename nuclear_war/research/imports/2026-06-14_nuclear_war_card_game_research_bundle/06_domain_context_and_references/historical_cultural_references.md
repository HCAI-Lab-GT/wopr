# Historical, cultural, and domain references

## Historical/cultural

- The LA Times 1986 article is a strong secondary source for the game's cult status, controversy, tournament culture, spinner-era components, sales, and black-humor framing.
- Museum records from The Strong/Google Arts & Culture and ORAU/Museum of Radiation and Radioactivity are useful object/archive references.
- The Origins Awards Hall of Fame source is useful for award/hobby significance.
- ICv2 provides modern publication-history context for *Nuclear Destruction* and the rights transition into Mr. B Games.

## Board-game design context

- InsideGMT's *Twilight Struggle* Cold War legacy article explicitly frames *Nuclear War* as a satirical Cold War board game, contrasting it with serious Cold War simulation designs.

## Professional/academic nuclear wargaming context

No strong evidence was found that Douglas Malewicki's *Nuclear War* card game is used as a serious professional nuclear escalation model. The sources mostly frame it as satire/hobby black humor.

Related professional/academic sources worth comparing:

- RAND, *Designing A Strange Game: A Nuclear Wargame for the 21st Century* - professional serious-game design for nuclear escalation learning.
- *Experimental wargaming at the nuclear threshold*, Journal of Peace Research - academic experimental wargaming on nuclear escalation and tailored nuclear capabilities.

These are not sources for *Nuclear War* rules. Use them to design simulation experiments or contrast satire-game dynamics with professional escalation models.
